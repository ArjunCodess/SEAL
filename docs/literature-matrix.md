# Literature and novelty assessment

Checked 10 November 2026. This is a targeted comparison, not a systematic review or a claim of exhaustive novelty. Titles, authors, and dates for arXiv entries were checked on their abstract pages; the two older DOI records were checked through Crossref. Summaries of the four recent monitor papers, EDDI, selective classification, Learn then Test, and Conformal Risk Control were checked against their abstracts. Secure-logging and feature-deletion entries are bibliographic anchors requiring full-text comparison before a novelty claim. No empirical result from these papers is reproduced here.

| AI control: Greenblatt et al., [AI Control: Improving Safety Despite Intentional Subversion](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.06942), 2023 | Evaluates safety and usefulness under intentional subversion and restricted oversight. | Compare evidence acquisition with audit/defer policies at the same measured total resource cost, including exhausted budgets. | Fixed evidence arms and sink outcomes only; no adaptive audit policy. |

## Contribution decision

The combination worth testing is adaptive recovery of authenticated evidence against selective concealment and workload exhaustion, with safety and legitimate completion measured at the same observed total cost. Each ingredient has close precedents. Neither this matrix nor SEAL establishes novelty or an advantage for that combination.

The current release supports a narrower empirical audit: four fixed observation/enforcement bundles, two live supervisors, frozen worker text, explicit distinction between unsafe publish advice and executed publication, and replayable synthetic sink outcomes. Its provenance arm changes both information and enforcement, so its zero executions do not isolate an evidence-acquisition effect.

The prospective go/no-go remains open. First measure full-retrieval cost on a development population. Continue selective acquisition only if that cost creates a real operational constraint and the frozen policy improves a prespecified endpoint over full retrieval and simple deferral at matched cost. Otherwise retain the audit-paper scope. A credit cap on collecting SEAL responses does not establish a constrained oversight budget.
